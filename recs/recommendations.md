## Bottleneck: Late detection

### A1. Non-specific serious symptoms (NSSC) fast-track pathway, run by the county's diagnostic unit

*Tags:* `bottleneck=late_detection` `goal3=G3-3,G3-1` `impact=High` `effort=Medium-High`

- **Pathway step:** Primary care → diagnostic work-up for vague symptoms (weight loss, fatigue, anaemia, GP 'gut feeling')
- **Problem:** Organ-specific fast tracks miss patients with vague symptoms. In Denmark, 62.8% of cancer patients after pathway rollout were not referred via a pathway and had longer diagnostic intervals (Jensen 2015). Poor-prognosis cancers (pancreas, liver, lung, stomach) often present vaguely (ROAD p.22).
- **Action:** Open one NSSC referral route: a GP referral form with criteria, a 1-day CT + lab package at the central hospital, and internal-medicine-led triage. Target: diagnosis or exclusion within 28 days (the NHS Faster Diagnosis Standard benchmark).
- **Who does what:** County chief medical officer (sponsor); Central-hospital internal medicine (clinical lead); Radiology (reserved CT slots); GPs (referral criteria); Nurse coordinator (booking, patient contact); ICT/Apotti or Lifecare/Kanta (referral form)
- **Effort / cost:** Medium–High. ESTIMATE: 1 FTE nurse coordinator (1 FTE nurse ≈ €46,902–€59,816/yr (sairaanhoitaja task-based €3,174 to total €4,048 per month, KT Oct 2025, × 12 × 1.2314 employer contributions, KT 2026)) + reallocated internal-medicine sessions + reserved CT slots (local unit costs needed; not verified).
- **KPIs moved:** Treatment within target times (≤6 wk referral→treatment) [goal3: G3-3]; Share diagnosed via NSSC route, conversion rate (% cancer) [process: county-internal]; Stage I–II share (Goal 2, secondary) [goal2: STRAT p.5]
- **Expected effect:** Precedents report cancer in 8–20% of referrals. Funen (DK) rose to ~6% of all new cancers found via this route. A Finnish yield is unknown until piloted.
- **Evidence:**
  - 5 English MDC pilots: 2,961 referrals, 241 cancers (8% conversion), broad range incl. rare cancers. [Chapman D et al. First results from five multidisciplinary diagnostic centre (MDC) projects for non-specific but concerning symptoms. Br J Cancer 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41416-020-0947-y)
  - Funen 2014–2021: 6,698 referrals, 20.2% had cancer; share of all new cancers found via the route rose from 3% to 6%. [Grønnemose RB et al. Risk of cancer and serious disease in Danish patients with urgent referral for serious non-specific symptoms, Funen 2014–2021. Br J Cancer 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38409600/)
  - Farsø DC 2020: 8% of 314 referrals had cancer; >¼ redirected. [Damhus CS et al. Diagnostic flow for all patients referred with non-specific symptoms of cancer to a diagnostic centre in Denmark. Eur J Gen Pract 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38179994/)
  - NHS Faster Diagnosis Standard: 28 days, target 75% to 31 Mar 2026, 80% from 1 Apr 2026. [NHS England. Cancer Waiting Times statistical release, March 2026 (Faster Diagnosis Standard 75% → 80%)](https://www.england.nhs.uk/statistics/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/10/Cancer-Waiting-Times-Statistical-Release-March-2026-Provider-based-Final.pdf)
- **Goal 3 mapping:** Patients get treatment within target times (THL Ohjaus 21/2016) (STRAT p.5; sub-goal 3.1 p.33); 5-year survival of poor-prognosis cancers reaches Nordic level (STRAT p.5) · *Goal 2 (secondary):* 70% stage I–II
- **Roadmap actions:** 2.5.2 (p.52–53) symptom-based models, direct test routes; 3.1.2 (p.56–57); Huonon ennusteen syövät (p.22–24)
- **First 90 days:** Wk 1–3: pick site and clinical lead; adapt Danish/UK NSSC criteria → Wk 4–6: reserve 2–4 CT slots/wk; create referral template → Wk 7–9: brief GPs in 3–5 health centres → Wk 10–13: go live and log referral date, diagnosis date, outcome
- **Risks / limitations:** Low conversion if criteria are loose; crowding out other imaging (Olsson 2020); Incidental findings increase workload; Small numbers in Q1: effect not measurable within 90 days

### A2. Symptomatic FIT in primary care to triage lower-GI symptoms

*Tags:* `bottleneck=late_detection` `goal3=G3-3,G3-2` `impact=Medium-High` `effort=Low`

- **Pathway step:** GP visit with lower-GI symptoms → colonoscopy referral
- **Problem:** Colonoscopy capacity is shared with the expanding national FIT screening programme (ages 56–74 by 2031). Unprioritised symptomatic referrals queue together.
- **Action:** Adopt the NICE/BSG rule: quantitative FIT for adults with lower-GI symptoms. FIT ≥10 µg Hb/g → suspected-cancer referral. Below that → safety-netting, and GPs may still refer on clinical concern.
- **Who does what:** County primary-care medical director; Lab (quantitative FIT analysis); Endoscopy unit (priority slots); GPs/nurses (test issue and safety-net)
- **Effort / cost:** Low–Medium. ESTIMATE: test kit + analysis per patient (price must come from the county lab contract; not verified). Uses the existing screening-lab FIT capability (SR_CRC).
- **KPIs moved:** Referral→treatment ≤6 wk for CRC [goal3: G3-3]; CRC mortality (part of −10%) [goal3: G3-2]; Colonoscopy waiting time; FIT-positive→colonoscopy days [process: county-internal]
- **Expected effect:** NICE judged FIT-guided referral cost-effective because colonoscopy capacity goes to those most likely to have CRC. We do not cite a quantified Finnish effect.
- **Evidence:**
  - FIT ≥10 µg Hb/g → suspected-cancer referral; safety-netting below threshold; FIT must not block referral if concern persists. [NICE HTG690: Quantitative FIT to guide colorectal cancer pathway referral in primary care (2023), rec 1.2–1.3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/HTG690/chapter/recommendations)
  - Joint UK guideline recommends FIT to prioritise symptomatic referrals. [ACPGBI & BSG. FIT in patients with signs or symptoms of suspected CRC: joint ACPGBI/BSG guideline. Gut 2022](https://www.acpgbi.org.uk/resources/1075/fit_in_patients_with_signs_or_symptoms_of_suspected_crc_a_joint_guideline_from_acpgbi_and_bsg/)
  - Finland's national CRC screening uses FIT, started 2022, covers ages 56–74 from 2031. [Suomen Syöpärekisteri: Suolistosyövän seulonta](https://syoparekisteri.fi/seulonta/suolistosyovan-seulonta/)
- **Goal 3 mapping:** Patients get treatment within target times (THL Ohjaus 21/2016) (STRAT p.5; sub-goal 3.1 p.33); Cancer mortality −10% (STRAT p.5) · *Goal 2 (secondary):* 70% stage I–II
- **Roadmap actions:** 2.5.2 (p.52–53) primary-care test responsibilities; 3.1.2 (p.56–57)
- **First 90 days:** Wk 1–4: agree threshold and pathway with the gastro unit and lab → Wk 5–8: EHR order set + safety-net recall list → Wk 9–13: launch in pilot health centres; track FIT volume and positivity
- **Risks / limitations:** False negatives if safety-netting fails; Return rates of samples may be lower in some groups (NICE research rec 1.5)

### A3. Lift FIT screening uptake in low-participation municipalities (extra reminder + multilingual outreach)

*Tags:* `bottleneck=late_detection` `goal3=G3-2` `impact=Medium` `effort=Low`

- **Pathway step:** Population screening invitation → test return → FIT-positive colonoscopy
- **Problem:** Strategy target: 80% participation in every county (Goal 2). Participation varies by area (Sotkanet 6103, regional values to come from the data analyst).
- **Action:** Add a second reminder (letter/SMS) to the screening-centre invitation flow for non-responders. Produce multilingual and plain-language material. A screening nurse calls every FIT-positive person to book colonoscopy.
- **Who does what:** County screening coordinator; Screening centre (invitations); Screening nurses; Communications + NGOs for language minorities
- **Effort / cost:** Low. ESTIMATE: postage/SMS per non-responder + design of material. National funding exists for language outreach: roadmap 1.3.1 €400,000 (ROAD p.33).
- **KPIs moved:** CRC mortality (part of −10%) [goal3: G3-2]; Colorectal screening participation % (Sotkanet 6103) [sotkanet: https://sotkanet.fi/sotkanet/fi/metadata/indicators/6103]; 80% participation per county (Goal 2, secondary) [goal2: STRAT p.5]
- **Expected effect:** In a Danish RCT (59,041 people), a four-stage invitation raised participation from 66.9% to 69.8% (+2.9 pp). FIT screening reduces CRC mortality by 10–40% (IARC, cited by the Cancer Registry).
- **Evidence:**
  - Four-stage invitation (pre-notification + 2 reminders): +2.9 pp (66.9%→69.8%); +4.0 pp at ages 50–59. [Larsen MB et al. The impact of pre-notifications and reminders on participation in colorectal cancer screening – RCT. Prev Med 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36057390/)
  - FIT screening reduces CRC mortality 10–40% (IARC Handbook 17, as reported by the Finnish Cancer Registry). [Suomen Syöpärekisteri: Suolistosyövän seulonta](https://syoparekisteri.fi/seulonta/suolistosyovan-seulonta/)
  - Roadmap target: participation of non-Finnish/Swedish speakers reaches the majority level. [Kansallinen syöpästrategia – Toimeenpanon tiekartta 2026–2031, action 1.3.1 p.33](https://files.hackjunction.app/resources/Kansallinen_syopastrategia_toimeenpanon_tiekartta_2026_2031.pdf)
- **Goal 3 mapping:** Cancer mortality −10% (STRAT p.5) · *Goal 2 (secondary):* 80% screening participation in every county
- **Roadmap actions:** 1.3.1 (p.33); 2.4.5 (p.50)
- **First 90 days:** Wk 1–2: pull participation by municipality (Sotkanet 6103, screening registry) → Wk 3–6: agree a reminder step with the screening centre → Wk 7–13: send reminders in the lowest-uptake municipalities; compare return rates
- **Risks / limitations:** More FIT-positives → colonoscopy demand; Effect is modest (+3 pp); mortality impact takes years

### A4. Record 'first symptom date' and flag repeat consultations in primary care (data + AI safety-net)

*Tags:* `bottleneck=late_detection` `goal3=G3-3` `impact=Medium` `effort=Medium`

- **Pathway step:** Repeated GP/nurse contacts before referral
- **Problem:** Strategy: data from first symptom to diagnosis are not collected systematically, and stage-bearing clinical notifications cover only ~40% of cases (STRAT p.30). In MDC data, more GP consultations before referral weakened the association with cancer (Chapman 2020).
- **Action:** Add a structured 'cancer suspicion / first symptom date' field to the GP referral. Run a monthly EHR query flagging ≥3 contacts in 90 days for red-flag codes (anaemia, weight loss, haematuria) for GP review. This is a pilot of AI-assisted early detection as the strategy envisages.
- **Who does what:** County CMIO / ICT (EHR field, query); Primary-care medical director; GPs (review flag list); Data analyst (evaluation)
- **Effort / cost:** Medium. ESTIMATE: EHR change request (vendor cost unknown, ask the county ICT) + ~0.2 FTE analyst. National support: roadmap 3.5.3 €1.14 M (ROAD p.74).
- **KPIs moved:** Treatment within target times (G3-3), extended upstream to first symptom [goal3: G3-3]; % referrals with first-symptom date recorded [process: county-internal]
- **Expected effect:** Mainly a measurement fix that makes delays visible. No direct outcome evidence verified for the flag itself. Mark as experimental.
- **Evidence:**
  - First-symptom-to-diagnosis data not collected systematically; stage notifications ~40% coverage. [Kansallinen syöpästrategia 2026–2035 (STM), p.30](https://files.hackjunction.app/resources/Kansallinen_syopastrategia_2026_2035_FINAL.pdf)
  - Target state: health IT and AI used to support early detection; collect symptom-to-diagnosis data. [Kansallinen syöpästrategia 2026–2035 (STM), p.30](https://files.hackjunction.app/resources/Kansallinen_syopastrategia_2026_2035_FINAL.pdf)
  - GP 'clinical suspicion' strong predictor; more prior GP consultations → weaker association with cancer. [Chapman D et al. First results from five multidisciplinary diagnostic centre (MDC) projects for non-specific but concerning symptoms. Br J Cancer 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41416-020-0947-y)
- **Goal 3 mapping:** Patients get treatment within target times (THL Ohjaus 21/2016) (STRAT p.5; sub-goal 3.1 p.33) · *Goal 2 (secondary):* 2.5 symptom-to-diagnosis data
- **Roadmap actions:** 3.5.3 (p.73–74) digital tools and AI; 4.1.1 (p.77) structured recording; 3.2.2 (p.60)
- **First 90 days:** Wk 1–4: define field + codes with GPs → Wk 5–10: ICT build/test → Wk 11–13: first monthly flag list; audit 50 flagged cases
- **Risks / limitations:** Alert fatigue; Data protection / secondary-use rules (toisiolaki); No proven outcome effect yet

## Bottleneck: Primary → specialist referral

### B1. Regional suspected-cancer referral pathway (pakkeforløb-style) for 3–5 common cancers

*Tags:* `bottleneck=referral` `goal3=G3-3,G3-1` `impact=High` `effort=Medium`

- **Pathway step:** GP referral → specialist acceptance → first diagnostic visit
- **Problem:** Finland has no cancer-specific pathway times in force. The only reference is THL Ohjaus 21/2016 (6 weeks referral→treatment), which is marked outdated and has no lung-specific limit. Cancer-specific times are due 2026–2028 (ROAD 3.1.2, p.57).
- **Action:** The county adopts written referral criteria plus a 'syöpäepäily' (suspected cancer) referral flag for lung, colorectal, breast, prostate and one poor-prognosis cancer. The receiving unit books the first diagnostic step within a fixed number of days. Timestamps follow THL 21/2016.
- **Who does what:** County oncology/central hospital clinical directors; Primary-care medical director; Referral-handling unit; FICAN regional centre (alignment)
- **Effort / cost:** Medium. ESTIMATE: mostly staff time (working groups, GP training). National funding is earmarked for pathways: €5 M/yr covering 2.5.2 and 3.1.2 (ROAD p.55).
- **KPIs moved:** % treated ≤6 wk from referral (THL 21/2016) [goal3: G3-3]; Referral arrival → first visit (days, median) [process: THL21 time points]; Median waiting time, specialised care (Sotkanet 5083, context only) [sotkanet: https://sotkanet.fi/sotkanet/fi/metadata/indicators/5083]
- **Expected effect:** Denmark: median diagnostic interval −17 days after pathway rollout (49→32 days unadjusted). Northern Sweden CRC: 47→29 days. Effects concentrate in patients actually referred into pathways.
- **Evidence:**
  - Median diagnostic interval 17 days shorter after CPP implementation (adjusted); unadjusted 49→35→32 days. [Jensen H et al. Diagnostic intervals before and after implementation of cancer patient pathways. BMC Cancer 2015](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12885-015-1317-7)
  - Patients not referred via a pathway had longer intervals; stage distribution did not change overall. [Jensen H et al. Tumour stage and implementation of standardised cancer patient pathways. Br J Gen Pract 2016](https://bjgp.org/content/bjgp/66/647/e434.full.pdf)
  - Northern Sweden CRC: median diagnostic interval 47→29 days after SVF, except right-sided colon. [Fjällström P et al. Reduction in the diagnostic interval after the introduction of cancer patient pathways for colorectal cancer in northern Sweden. Scand J Prim Health Care 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37450480/)
  - SVF: waiting times shortened for first pathways; crowding out must be managed. [Schmidt I et al. The national program on standardized cancer care pathways in Sweden: observations half way through. Health Policy 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30075866/)
  - Roadmap expects standardised referral criteria for ≥5 cancers and county pilots in 2026–2028. [Kansallinen syöpästrategia – Toimeenpanon tiekartta 2026–2031, 2.5.2 p.53](https://files.hackjunction.app/resources/Kansallinen_syopastrategia_toimeenpanon_tiekartta_2026_2031.pdf)
- **Goal 3 mapping:** Patients get treatment within target times (THL Ohjaus 21/2016) (STRAT p.5; sub-goal 3.1 p.33); 5-year survival of poor-prognosis cancers reaches Nordic level (STRAT p.5) · *Goal 2 (secondary):* Referral criteria in all national pathways (2.5)
- **Roadmap actions:** 2.5.2 (p.52–53); 3.1.2 (p.56–57); 3.1.4 (p.58)
- **First 90 days:** Wk 1–3: choose 3 cancers; copy Danish/Swedish/Norwegian criteria as drafts → Wk 4–8: clinical consensus; create referral flag in EHR → Wk 9–13: launch; weekly report of flagged referrals and days to first visit
- **Risks / limitations:** Crowding out non-pathway patients (Olsson 2020); Over-referral strains imaging; Pathway-outside patients may wait longer (Jensen 2015)

### B2. Named cancer-pathway coordinator (nurse navigator) from referral to first treatment

*Tags:* `bottleneck=referral` `goal3=G3-3` `impact=Medium-High` `effort=Low`

- **Pathway step:** Referral receipt → tests → MDT → treatment start
- **Problem:** The roadmap notes that if the patient cannot demand urgent care or coordination is poor, total time 'may become very long' (ROAD p.13).
- **Action:** Appoint 1–2 nurse coordinators at the central hospital. They own every flagged suspected-cancer referral: booking tests, chasing results, one phone number for patient and GP, and escalating breaches of the 6-week target.
- **Who does what:** Central-hospital nursing director; Nurse coordinators; Oncology/surgical units; GP (receives status)
- **Effort / cost:** Low–Medium. 1 FTE nurse ≈ €46,902–€59,816/yr (sairaanhoitaja task-based €3,174 to total €4,048 per month, KT Oct 2025, × 12 × 1.2314 employer contributions, KT 2026) → 2 FTE ≈ €93,804–€119,632/yr.
- **KPIs moved:** % treated ≤6 wk from referral [goal3: G3-3]; Use of named-coordinator model (roadmap 1.2.3 indicator) [roadmap: ROAD p.32]
- **Expected effect:** Norway introduced a cancer pathway coordinator role with its pathways. US navigation trial: OR 2.12 for timely diagnostic resolution at 365 days, with no difference at 60/180 days. The effect on Finnish waiting times is unverified.
- **Evidence:**
  - Norwegian CPPs introduced a cancer pathway coordinator to manage referrals, book appointments and inform patients. [Nilssen Y et al. Compliance with recommended cancer patient pathway timeframes ... Norway 2015–2016. BMC Cancer 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12885-022-09306-9)
  - Patient navigation: aOR 2.12 (1.36–3.29) for timely diagnostic resolution at 365 days; no significant effect at 60/180 days. [Battaglia TA et al. The impact of patient navigation on the delivery of diagnostic breast cancer care (PNRP). Breast Cancer Res Treat 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27432417/)
  - Roadmap 1.2.3: named coordinating worker model; guidance 2026–2028, county adoption 2029–2031. [Kansallinen syöpästrategia – Toimeenpanon tiekartta 2026–2031, p.32](https://files.hackjunction.app/resources/Kansallinen_syopastrategia_toimeenpanon_tiekartta_2026_2031.pdf)
- **Goal 3 mapping:** Patients get treatment within target times (THL Ohjaus 21/2016) (STRAT p.5; sub-goal 3.1 p.33)
- **Roadmap actions:** 1.2.3 (p.32); 3.1.2 (p.56–57)
- **First 90 days:** Wk 1–4: role description, redeploy 1 experienced nurse → Wk 5–8: coordinator list of all flagged referrals; breach escalation rule → Wk 9–13: report weekly breaches and causes
- **Risks / limitations:** Single-person dependency; Effect unproven in Finland; the US evidence comes from a different context

### B3. Asynchronous e-consultation (GP ↔ specialist) before or instead of referral

*Tags:* `bottleneck=referral` `goal3=G3-3` `impact=Medium` `effort=Low`

- **Pathway step:** GP uncertainty → referral decision
- **Problem:** Rejected or incomplete referrals and unclear cases add loops. Distances in Finland are long (>400 km to a central hospital for some, ROAD p.13).
- **Action:** Specialists in key tumour areas answer GP e-consults within 2 working days, converting to a pathway referral when needed.
- **Who does what:** Specialty units (rota of answering specialists); GPs; ICT (consult message type in EHR)
- **Effort / cost:** Low–Medium. ESTIMATE: specialist time ~ a few hours/week per specialty (not costed). Reuses existing EHR messaging; vendor cost unknown.
- **KPIs moved:** % treated ≤6 wk from referral [goal3: G3-3]; E-consult response time; % resolved without a visit [process: county-internal]
- **Expected effect:** NYC safety-net: 13% of requests resolved electronically, wait time −8.2 days (−13.3%). Systematic review: timely specialist input, well received by GPs.
- **Evidence:**
  - 27 studies: e-consults provide timely specialty access and are well received by PCPs. [Vimalananda VG et al. Electronic consultations (e-consults) to improve access to specialty care: systematic review. J Telemed Telecare 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25995331/)
  - 13% of requests resolved electronically; mean wait 61.0→54.1 days (adjusted −8.2 days). [Gaye M et al. Association of eConsult implementation with access to specialist care in a large urban safety-net system. JAMA Health Forum 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35977310/)
- **Goal 3 mapping:** Patients get treatment within target times (THL Ohjaus 21/2016) (STRAT p.5; sub-goal 3.1 p.33)
- **Roadmap actions:** 3.5.3 (p.73–74) digital tools, remote consultations; 3.4 (p.39) primary care can consult geriatric-oncology team
- **First 90 days:** Wk 1–4: pick 2 specialties; define a 2-day response → Wk 5–13: run and track volume, response time, conversions
- **Risks / limitations:** Evidence not cancer-specific; Specialist time is not freed automatically

### B4. Direct GP access to diagnostic imaging (straight-to-test) for selected symptoms

*Tags:* `bottleneck=referral` `goal3=G3-3` `impact=Low-Medium` `effort=Medium`

- **Pathway step:** GP → imaging without a specialist visit
- **Problem:** Each extra specialist visit adds days. The roadmap explicitly mentions direct test routes (ROAD p.52).
- **Action:** Allow GPs to order chest CT or abdominal US directly for defined symptoms, with radiology reporting within 1 week (THL 21/2016 target).
- **Who does what:** Radiology; Primary care; Pathway group (criteria)
- **Effort / cost:** Medium. ESTIMATE: imaging volume shift. Unit costs from the county price list (not verified).
- **KPIs moved:** Imaging report ≤1 week (THL 21/2016 sub-target) [goal3: G3-3]
- **Expected effect:** MIXED evidence: a Danish cluster RCT of direct GP access to low-dose CT did not significantly change stage or time to diagnosis.
- **Evidence:**
  - Direct GP access to LDCT: no significant effect on stage or time to diagnosis. [Guldbrandt LM et al. The effect of direct access to CT scan in early lung cancer detection: cluster-randomised trial. BMC Cancer 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26608727/)
  - THL target: radiology report within 1 week of request in suspected cancer. [THL Ohjaus 21/2016 Hoitoonpääsyn seuranta syövän hoidossa, printed p.13](https://www.julkari.fi/items/0424a55a-f159-406f-8883-5dfdad4d6772)
- **Goal 3 mapping:** Patients get treatment within target times (THL Ohjaus 21/2016) (STRAT p.5; sub-goal 3.1 p.33)
- **Roadmap actions:** 2.5.2 (p.52) suorat tutkimusreitit
- **First 90 days:** Only as part of B1 criteria; measure before scaling
- **Risks / limitations:** Evidence does not show outcome benefit; Imaging overuse

### B5. Functional status (G8 / Clinical Frailty Scale) in every suspected-cancer referral for patients ≥75

*Tags:* `bottleneck=referral` `goal3=G3-3` `impact=Low-Medium` `effort=Low`

- **Pathway step:** GP referral content → specialist test planning for older patients
- **Problem:** The number of cancer patients over 75 is growing fast, and functional assessment is often missing (STRAT p.39).
- **Action:** Add mandatory G8/CFS fields to the referral template for patients aged 75+. Specialists use them to plan tests and avoid futile invasive work-up.
- **Who does what:** Primary-care GPs/nurses; ICT (template); Geriatrics/oncology
- **Effort / cost:** Low. ESTIMATE: template change + 1-hour training. National: roadmap 3.4.2 €1 M (ROAD p.69).
- **KPIs moved:** % treated ≤6 wk (older patients) [goal3: G3-3]; Functional status included in referrals (roadmap 3.4.2 indicator) [roadmap: ROAD p.69]
- **Expected effect:** Supports appropriate, faster decisions. No quantified waiting-time effect verified.

## Bottleneck: Diagnosis → treatment

### C1. Monthly 'time-to-treatment' dashboard on THL 21/2016 time points, with breach alerts

*Tags:* `bottleneck=diagnosis_to_treatment` `goal3=G3-3` `impact=High` `effort=Medium`

- **Pathway step:** Referral arrival → first visit → treatment decision → first treatment
- **Problem:** The G3-3 target exists (6 weeks), but there is no public regional reporting. Norway found the treatment decision→start step to be the main delay for all cancers studied.
- **Action:** Extract the four THL 21/2016 dates from the EHR/Hilmo for 16 cancers. Publish median and % ≤6 weeks per cancer and per municipality monthly to the county board. The coordinator (B2) gets an alert at day 28 without a treatment decision.
- **Who does what:** County knowledge management / data analyst; ICT (data extraction); Oncology clinical director (owner of action); County board (oversight)
- **Effort / cost:** Medium. ESTIMATE: ~0.5 FTE analyst for 3 months + BI tool already licensed (assumption). (ROAD 3.2.2, p.60, funds national FICAN/THL peer review, not county work.)
- **KPIs moved:** % treated ≤6 wk from referral, per cancer & area [goal3: G3-3]; Treatment decision → first treatment (days) [process: THL21]
- **Expected effect:** Makes the Goal 3 indicator visible. Norway's national monitoring located bottlenecks (decision→treatment, travel outside the home trust). Outcome effect depends on the actions triggered.
- **Evidence:**
  - THL 21/2016 defines time points: referral arrival, first visit, treatment-queue date, first treatment; max 6 weeks referral→first treatment. [THL Ohjaus 21/2016 Hoitoonpääsyn seuranta syövän hoidossa, PDF p.5, printed p.13](https://www.julkari.fi/items/0424a55a-f159-406f-8883-5dfdad4d6772)
  - Norway 2015–16: decision→treatment was the main delay; travelling outside the home health trust prolonged waits. [Nilssen Y et al. Compliance with recommended cancer patient pathway timeframes ... Norway 2015–2016. BMC Cancer 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12885-022-09306-9)
  - Roadmap 3.2.2–3.2.3: peer benchmarking and steering mechanisms to react to deviations. [Kansallinen syöpästrategia – Toimeenpanon tiekartta 2026–2031, p.60–61](https://files.hackjunction.app/resources/Kansallinen_syopastrategia_toimeenpanon_tiekartta_2026_2031.pdf)
- **Goal 3 mapping:** Patients get treatment within target times (THL Ohjaus 21/2016) (STRAT p.5; sub-goal 3.1 p.33)
- **Roadmap actions:** 3.1.4 (p.58); 3.2.2 (p.60); 3.2.3 (p.61); 3.5.3 (p.73–74)
- **First 90 days:** Wk 1–4: data extraction spec (THL 21/2016 codes) → Wk 5–8: baseline for last 12 months → Wk 9–13: first board report + alerts
- **Risks / limitations:** Recording quality of queue/decision dates; Small numbers per municipality → suppress cells <5

### C2. Pathologist-initiated reflex biomarker testing for NSCLC (and other agreed tumours)

*Tags:* `bottleneck=diagnosis_to_treatment` `goal3=G3-3,G3-1` `impact=Medium-High` `effort=Low`

- **Pathway step:** Histology → molecular result → first systemic treatment
- **Problem:** Roadmap: molecular tests are done in many labs, answers are not ready fast enough for treatment planning, and there are notable regional differences (ROAD p.88).
- **Action:** Pathology automatically orders the agreed biomarker panel at diagnosis of non-squamous NSCLC, without waiting for an oncologist request. Results go via electronic channels.
- **Who does what:** County pathology lab; Thoracic oncology; Lab procurement (send-out contract); MTB/regional centre
- **Effort / cost:** Low–Medium. ESTIMATE: no extra tests per eligible patient (testing moves earlier). Some extra tests may follow in patients who would not have been tested. Panel price from the lab contract (not verified). National: roadmap 4.3.3 €400,000 (ROAD p.88).
- **KPIs moved:** % treated within target times (lung) [goal3: G3-3]; Lung cancer 5-yr survival to Nordic level [goal3: G3-1]; Biopsy → molecular result (days) [process: county-internal]
- **Expected effect:** Toronto: median time to treatment 34→22 days (excluding intentional delays), time to optimal first-line therapy 36→24 days, fewer failed tests.
- **Evidence:**
  - Reflex EGFR/ALK: time to optimal first-line therapy 36→24 days (p=.036); TTT 34→22 days excluding intentional delays; failed EGFR tests 14%→4%. [Cheema PK et al. Impact of reflex EGFR/ALK testing on time to treatment of patients with advanced nonsquamous NSCLC. J Oncol Pract 2017](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JOP.2016.014019)
  - Molecular testing done in several labs, low utilisation, answers not fast enough; regional differences. [Kansallinen syöpästrategia – Toimeenpanon tiekartta 2026–2031, 4.3.3 p.87–88](https://files.hackjunction.app/resources/Kansallinen_syopastrategia_toimeenpanon_tiekartta_2026_2031.pdf)
  - G3-1 targets lung cancer 5-yr survival +>14 pp to Nordic level. [Kansallinen syöpästrategia 2026–2035 (STM), p.5](https://files.hackjunction.app/resources/Kansallinen_syopastrategia_2026_2035_FINAL.pdf)
- **Goal 3 mapping:** Patients get treatment within target times (THL Ohjaus 21/2016) (STRAT p.5; sub-goal 3.1 p.33); 5-year survival of poor-prognosis cancers reaches Nordic level (STRAT p.5)
- **Roadmap actions:** 4.3.2 (p.87); 4.3.3 (p.87–88); 4.3.4 (p.88)
- **First 90 days:** Wk 1–4: agree reflex criteria and panel with oncology → Wk 5–8: lab SOP; send-out logistics → Wk 9–13: go live; measure turnaround time
- **Risks / limitations:** Cost shift to the pathology budget; Tissue adequacy; Possible conflict of interest perception (AZ is a sponsor): use guideline-based panels

### C3. Fixed weekly MDT slots with pre-reserved treatment-start capacity

*Tags:* `bottleneck=diagnosis_to_treatment` `goal3=G3-3` `impact=Medium` `effort=Medium`

- **Pathway step:** Diagnosis complete → MDT decision → surgery/oncology slot
- **Problem:** Norway found the decision→treatment interval to be the main delay. THL issued 2025 guidance on cancer MDT meetings (THL21 annex 2).
- **Action:** Schedule MDTs weekly per tumour group (remote participation for regional hospitals). Each MDT ends with a booked treatment date. Keep 'pathway' surgical/oncology slots that are released if unused 72 h ahead.
- **Who does what:** Surgery and oncology unit heads; MDT coordinators; Theatre planning
- **Effort / cost:** Medium. ESTIMATE: capacity reallocation, not new money. Risk of idle slots (cost unknown).
- **KPIs moved:** Treatment decision → first treatment ≤ target; % ≤6 wk overall [goal3: G3-3]
- **Expected effect:** Targets the step Norway identified as the main delay. No verified effect size for slot reservation itself.
- **Evidence:**
  - Decision→treatment main source of delay for all cancers studied (Norway). [Nilssen Y et al. Compliance with recommended cancer patient pathway timeframes ... Norway 2015–2016. BMC Cancer 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12885-022-09306-9)
  - THL time-points guidance incl. 2025 annex on multidisciplinary planning meetings. [THL Ohjaus 21/2016 Hoitoonpääsyn seuranta syövän hoidossa, Liite 2 (2025)](https://www.julkari.fi/items/0424a55a-f159-406f-8883-5dfdad4d6772)
  - Adjuvant therapy should start ≤4 weeks after surgery. [THL Ohjaus 21/2016 Hoitoonpääsyn seuranta syövän hoidossa, printed p.13](https://www.julkari.fi/items/0424a55a-f159-406f-8883-5dfdad4d6772)
- **Goal 3 mapping:** Patients get treatment within target times (THL Ohjaus 21/2016) (STRAT p.5; sub-goal 3.1 p.33)
- **Roadmap actions:** 3.1.2 (p.56–57); Huonon ennusteen syövät: hoitoketjut ja yhteistyö (p.24)
- **First 90 days:** Wk 1–4: map current MDT cadence and decision→treatment days (from C1) → Wk 5–13: pilot in one tumour group
- **Risks / limitations:** Crowding out of benign surgery (Olsson 2020)

### C4. Bring treatment and follow-up closer to home for remote residents (remote visits, shared care with local hospitals)

*Tags:* `bottleneck=diagnosis_to_treatment` `goal3=G3-3,G3-2` `impact=Medium` `effort=Medium`

- **Pathway step:** Treatment start/continuation for patients living far from the centre
- **Problem:** Outcomes are worse beyond 50 km from the treatment unit, and palliative radiotherapy use is lower beyond 100 km (ROAD p.13). Norway: travelling outside the home trust prolonged waits.
- **Action:** Use remote oncology visits and give oral therapy and follow-up in the nearest hospital or health centre where clinically safe. Arrange patient travel at the time of the MDT.
- **Who does what:** Oncology unit; Local hospitals/health centres; Kela travel coordination; ICT (remote visits)
- **Effort / cost:** Medium. ESTIMATE: offset partly by lower travel reimbursement (not quantified).
- **KPIs moved:** % treated ≤6 wk by distance band [goal3: G3-3]; Mortality gap by area [goal3: G3-2]
- **Expected effect:** Equity effect for remote areas. Magnitude unverified.
- **Evidence:**
  - Worse outcomes >50 km; less palliative RT >100 km; recommends remote/decentralised care. [Kansallinen syöpästrategia – Toimeenpanon tiekartta 2026–2031, p.13](https://files.hackjunction.app/resources/Kansallinen_syopastrategia_toimeenpanon_tiekartta_2026_2031.pdf)
  - Travelling outside the resident health trust prolonged waiting time. [Nilssen Y et al. Compliance with recommended cancer patient pathway timeframes ... Norway 2015–2016. BMC Cancer 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12885-022-09306-9)
- **Goal 3 mapping:** Patients get treatment within target times (THL Ohjaus 21/2016) (STRAT p.5; sub-goal 3.1 p.33); Cancer mortality −10% (STRAT p.5)
- **Roadmap actions:** 3.5.3 (p.73–74); Alueellinen eriarvoisuus (p.12–13)
- **First 90 days:** Wk 1–6: identify patients >100 km in C1 data → Wk 7–13: remote-visit pilot for follow-ups
- **Risks / limitations:** Safety of decentralised therapy needs local competence
