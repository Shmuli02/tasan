# WHAT WINS — Junction health / pharma-partner challenges, and these judges

Short research note for the pitch, 14 Nov 2026. Every claim has a URL. Where the evidence is thin, this note says so. It is **not** a substitute for the official AstraZeneca brief in `/workspace/junction-new/CHALLENGE.txt`, which is the strongest evidence of what *these* judges will score.

## 1. How Junction partner challenges are actually judged

Junction's own partner-info deck says partner-challenge winners are chosen by the partner, using *the partner's own criteria*. The examples it gives are "1) Innovation, 2) Business Viability, 3) User Experience". The main-prize path (top-5, grand jury) is separate.

- Source: https://www.hackjunction.com/junction-infodeck.pdf

**Implication for us:** Hanna Rusila, Laura Aaltonen (Marketing) and Joanna Tuormaa (Business Excellence) are the whole game at 14:00 on Sunday. They published unusually specific criteria — Actionability 30, Insight 25, Data quality & transparency 20, Execution 15, Clarity 10 — plus four minimum requirements. Those override Junction's generic three. Treat "innovation" and "AI-powered" as table stakes, not the scoring centre.

## 2. What past Junction winners were praised for (health and otherwise)

Public, judge-facing commentary is surprisingly scarce. Junction's official winner posts are often name lists, not rubrics. The useful fragments:

**"Practically ready to deploy", strong UX, real operational job — Junction 2017 main prize (Signvision).** The official Junction blog says the main judges praised Signvision's technical implementation and "impressive user experience", that it "was said [to] have real potential and that it was practically ready to be deployed." The product mapped a maintenance job (find damaged traffic signs, build a service route), not a tech demo for its own sake.

- https://blog.hackjunction.com/a-mix-of-machine-learning-and-cool-ux-won-junction-2017-15963c0ace3b

**A well-defined user need + a prototype that actually worked + a good presentation — Movesense / Health Tech, Junction 2018.** Movesense's own write-up of its challenge winner ("Smart labour assistant") is one of the few sponsor comments that names *why*: "The concept started from a well-defined user need, the prototype worked well, demonstrated cross-platform use … had great user experience, and even the presentation was excellent."

- https://www.movesense.com/news/2018/11/movesense-shined-in-junction-hackathon/

**A practical tool for a named clinician, not a concept video — Varian (cancer radiotherapy) at Junction 2019.** Varian Medical Systems Finland set "Effective Radiation Therapy Plan Review". Makers UPV won with a *working web app* that let an oncologist open a plan anywhere, compare DVHs and organ doses, check robustness, and share a checklist with the physicist or patient. The university write-up says the challenge goal was to cut review time with technology that fitted the clinical environment. This is the closest past Junction *cancer-care* partner challenge I found.

- Official winners list: https://blog.hackjunction.com/the-winners-of-junction-2019-de539036dc3f
- UPV account of the winning product: https://www.upv.es/noticias-upv/noticia-11673-makers-upv-tri-es.html

**Insight from data that changes how you see the patient group — Sitra "Handle with Care", Junction 2017.** Sitra's own story on Diabetes Profiling (the winner) emphasises a data insight: diabetes is not only type 1 vs type 2; clustering lab values could change how you think about course and risk. That is "insight", not a prettier dashboard of known averages. Evidence quality: a sponsor press note, not a judging rubric, but it is first-party.

- https://arkisto.sitra.fi/uutiset/vahista-unista-viis-diabetes-profiling-voittoon-sitran-junction-haasteessa/

**Low-friction product for a named non-technical user, built with mentors — Aalto "Aging with AI", Junction 2025.** ApprenticeCircle+ won; Aalto's article highlights a voice-first design, onboarding via a 10-minute phone call (so older users are not forced through a fiddly app), a lived-experience problem, and conversation with the partner's researchers. Mentors are described as impressed by students who bounced ideas with them.

- https://www.aalto.fi/en/news/aalto-students-and-researchers-shape-together-how-we-age-with-ai-at-junction-hackathon-2025

**If the partner already knows what they want, build that — don't "Figma-ton" it.** A Junction 2024 participant write-up (Kone challenge, not health; one person's view) argues that partner challenges split into "vague theme" vs "they know the product they want"; that you can often find out which by asking the stand; and that a vague challenge can sometimes be won with a polished concept, but a specified challenge rewards actually implementing the ask. Our brief is unusually specified. Treat this source as informal and single-author, but the distinction is useful.

- https://kuchta.dev/posts/junction-2024/

## 3. Recent Finnish health-partner challenges at Junction (2024–2025)

I found **no official Junction blog write-up** that explains why the 2024 or 2025 private-clinic / pharma health challenges were awarded. What exists is team LinkedIn, which is self-reported. Use it only as a weak signal.

- **Junction 2025, Pfizer × Lääkärikeskus Aava, "Head Start".** Team posts say the first-place project was a predictive migraine companion (referred to as Ease in secondary summaries of those posts), with MigrainNot reporting second place. This shows a Finnish private clinic and a pharma company co-sponsoring a *named clinical problem* and rewarding a user-facing, working idea — not a health-system strategy tool. It is only partly analogous to our county-director brief.
  - https://www.linkedin.com/posts/aarnikonola_great-weekend-at-junction-2025-our-team-activity-7396245968893063171-ly8O
  - https://www.linkedin.com/posts/krisjanis-mezulis-758b2a59_junction2025-hackathon-ai-activity-7396237960196632576-iM-F
- **Junction 2024, Lääkärikeskus Aava.** Multiple team posts describe Aava challenge placements (e.g. Balance Hub / workplace wellbeing). I could not find an official "this is why they won" note.

**Evidence is thin here.** Do not overfit to migraine or wellbeing apps.

## 4. AstraZeneca hackathons — thin evidence, one useful echo

I found **no public winner write-up of a previous AstraZeneca challenge at Junction**. I also did not find this 2026 "Equal Care Across Finland" challenge described on the public 2026.hackjunction.com marketing pages I searched. For "what AZ hackathon judges reward", we should not pretend there is a rich record.

What I could find, with limits:

- **Wellcome Genome Campus BioDataHack, 2018, AstraZeneca challenge.** Oxford/Cambridge students won AZ's challenge ("map drug-disease relationships using machine learning and/or AI") with TINDR, a method plus an R package, and the university note says they would "continue to develop their solution … with the help of mentors at the company" and were "in talks with AstraZeneca to continue their work." That is the closest documented AZ-hackathon "what happened after". It rhymes with our brief's **Beyond Junction** line (a follow-up, ideally with a Finnish wellbeing services county).
  - https://www.chg.ox.ac.uk/news/dphil-gms-students-win-astrazeneca-challenge-at-biodatahack
- **AstraZeneca R&D IT GenAI hackathon (2024) and other internal AZ events.** Public LinkedIn posts exist (organisers saying they judged or hosted). They do not name winners or criteria in a form I could cite as judging guidance. Evidence: too thin to use.
- **Thematic, not a hackathon:** AstraZeneca US Oncology leadership has argued publicly that AI should be used to expose care disparities and that "everyone, regardless of their ZIP code, deserves access". This is branded thought-leadership (PharmaVoice interview, circulated on LinkedIn), not a judging rubric, but it is consistent with the equity frame of this brief.
  - https://www.linkedin.com/posts/mohitmanrao_az-oncology-chief-says-ai-can-help-solve-activity-7196101951154016256-y0YZ

## 5. What business and marketing judges tend to reward

Our Sunday panel is Marketing × 2 and Business Excellence × 1. None of the published Sunday judges is a clinician (Medical Affairs mentor Kaisa Kaitila is listed for Saturday, not for Sunday judging). That is in the brief itself.

I did **not** find interviews with Rusila, Aaltonen or Tuormaa about how they will judge. So the only first-party evidence is the brief they are judging against:

- "Recommend what to do. **This is the most important part.** Diagnosing the problem is not enough."
- Actionability is the heaviest criterion (30%).
- "A strong solution should make a complex situation clear and actionable for a **non-technical decision-maker in less than two minutes.**"
- Possible solution types include an AI analyst, a dashboard *with recommendations*, an early-warning system, or a simulation of an intervention — the format is open, the action is not optional.
- They already want a path **beyond Junction**, "ideally through a follow-up project in collaboration with a Finnish wellbeing services county."
- They already warn that cancer-care data is limited, and that facing the data gap is part of the challenge — so hiding limitations will not impress a transparency criterion worth 20%.

Generic "what healthcare-hackathon judges want" articles exist, but I am not leaning on them: they are not about these judges or this brief. The brief is enough, and it is unusually clear.

## 6. Ten implications for our pitch

1. **Lead with a Monday action, not with the model.** Actionability is 30% and the brief calls recommendations the most important part. If the first minute is an architecture diagram, we have already spent the Clarity budget.

2. **Name the user: a hyvinvointialue director (or a health-centre lead).** Past health-partner winners that we can document (Varian 2019, Aalto 2025, Movesense 2018) start from a well-defined user and a job they already have. Our user is the person the brief names — a non-technical decision-maker in a county.

3. **One gap, one pathway step, one Goal 3 indicator, one ask.** Insight (25%) is about revealing a real gap *and* where it emerges. Several bottlenecks in parallel will read as a literature review.

4. **The live demo must be the story, and it must actually run.** Execution is only 15%, but a live-demo failure is a gate fail. Signvision was praised for being nearly deployable; Varian's winner was a working web app. Have an offline path and a backup recording. Slides alone are an official fail.

5. **Do the brief they wrote, not a more 'innovative' brief.** This partner challenge is specified (gap → pathway location → concrete recs → Goal 3 → sources → demo). Informal Junction advice (Kuchta 2024, single-author) and Junction's own "partner uses own criteria" rule both point the same way: implement the ask.

6. **Put a source under every number, and say what you cannot see.** Transparency is 20%. The brief already admits the data is thin; the strategy and the roadmap (Goal 3.2, strategy pp. 35–36; roadmap p. 102 on hard-to-estimate Goal 3 savings) admit the knowledge base is incomplete. Honesty here is on-strategy, not a weakness, provided we do not invent numbers to fill the hole.

7. **Quote Goal 3 in Finnish, with a page number, on screen.** Marketing judges can still tick a compliance box when they see the official wording. Do not pass off Goal 2 stage-at-diagnosis indicators as Goal 3.

8. **Close with Beyond Junction as a concrete county pilot, not a flattery line.** The only documented AZ hackathon "win aftermath" I found (BioDataHack 2018) valued continuation with the company. Our brief already offers the vehicle: a follow-up with one Finnish wellbeing services county. Make that the ask (90 days, one indicator, one action).

9. **Use Saturday's Medical Affairs mentor as a cited source, not as a substitute Sunday judge.** If Kaisa Kaitila (or Friday mentors) gives a usable insight, attribute it. Do not assume the Sunday panel will complete a clinical argument for us. Translate everything into operational language: owner, cost band, KPI, start date.

10. **Evidence of "what usually wins Junction health / AZ challenges" is otherwise thin — do not cargo-cult last year's migraine app, a Figma-only concept, or an AZ research method from 2018.** The record of judge commentary is short and often second-hand. When in doubt, re-read the official criteria and score ourselves against `SCORECARD.md`.
