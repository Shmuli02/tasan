# SCORECARD — Junction 2026, AstraZeneca "Equal Care Across Finland"

Harsh, usable judging sheet for our own work. Judging: Sun 15 Nov 2026 14:00 Europe/Helsinki, by AstraZeneca Marketing (Hanna Rusila, Laura Aaltonen) and Business Excellence (Joanna Tuormaa). They are not clinical judges. They will reward a clear "what a wellbeing services county should do on Monday" story, with sources on screen.

Official brief: `/workspace/junction-new/CHALLENGE.txt`. Official criteria weights below are taken from that brief, not invented.

**How to use this sheet.** Run the four gates first. If any gate fails, the entry is not competitive — record `GATE FAIL`, still fill the diagnostic scores, and list the top 5 fixes. Then score each criterion 0, 1, 3 or 5 (no 2s or 4s: if you are hesitating, it is not a 5).

**Formula.** `Score = (0.30×A + 0.25×I + 0.20×D + 0.15×E + 0.10×C) / 5 × 100`. Range 0–100.

| Band | Score | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Not competitive | GATE FAIL, or < 60 | A judge can dismiss us in the first minute |
| Competent | 60–74 | Meets the brief, will not win |
| Contender | 75–84 | In the prize conversation |
| Winning | 85–100 | Concrete Monday action, sourced gap, live demo, two-minute story |

## 1. Four minimum-requirement gates

Fail any one of these and we are out, regardless of how pretty the demo is. These are the official minimum requirements in the challenge brief, restated as pass/fail tests a scorer can apply in five minutes.

### G1 — Reliable evidence; every claim has a visible source

**Pass only if all of the following are true:**

- Every number, comparison and causal claim shown in the demo, the deck and the spoken pitch has a visible source (on-screen citation or footer: dataset + publisher + year + URL or indicator id).
- Sources are public data and/or clearly attributed AstraZeneca mentor insights. Mentor quotes are dated and named, not "experts say".
- Invented, imputed, synthetic or illustrative figures are labelled `EXAMPLE` or `PLACEHOLDER` in the same visual field as the number, not only in a footnote.
- A limitations statement exists (data lag, missing regions, ecological fallacy, no patient-level data) and is shown, not buried.

**Fail if:** any headline number is unsourced; a placeholder is presented as a finding; or "AI says" is treated as evidence.

### G2 — At least one Goal 3 indicator, named and quoted from the strategy

**Pass only if all of the following are true:**

- The solution covers at least one *seurantamittari* of **päätavoite 3**, "Varmistetaan yhdenvertainen ja vaikuttava syövänhoito", as printed on strategy p. 5 (or a sub-goal 3.1–3.5 measurement bullet quoted from pp. 33–42). See section 4.
- The indicator is *named in Finnish as in the PDF*, quoted, and cited with page number, in the demo and in the deck.
- The team does not pass this gate by using a **Goal 2** indicator only (screening participation, share diagnosed in stages I–II, preventable cancers). Those are real, but they are not Goal 3. A detection-centred story must still display a Goal 3 indicator (typically treatment-start within target times, poor-prognosis 5-year survival, or 10% mortality reduction).

**Fail if:** "we address Goal 3" is asserted without a named mittari, or the quoted line is from Goal 1, 2, 4, or only from the cross-cutting yhdenvertaisuus row without also naming a Goal 3 mittari.

### G3 — Concrete, implementable recommendations — not just analysis

**Pass only if each headline recommendation states all five:**

1. **Who** (named role: e.g. johtajaylilääkäri, terveysaseman esimies, FICAN regional, hoitotyön johtaja)
2. **Does what** (a verb you could put in a Monday morning email)
3. **Where** (named hyvinvointialue, or "every terveysasema in [county]", not "Finland")
4. **By when** (a date or a 30/90-day window inside 2026–2031)
5. **Expected effect and KPI** (tied to the chosen Goal 3 indicator)

Preferably also: a cost or effort band, mapped to a numbered toimenpide in the implementation roadmap 2026–2031 (Goal 3 starts at roadmap p. 54; Goal 3 funding need 49 730 000 €, roadmap annex p. 106).

**Fail if:** the recs are "improve referrals", "raise awareness", "use AI", or a problem diagnosis with no owner.

### G4 — Working live demonstration

**Pass only if:** a judge can see the solution run live in front of them. Slides, Figma, or a recorded video *alone* fail (a silent backup recording is allowed *in addition* to the live demo). The demo must use the team's real findings or clearly labelled placeholders, and survive being clicked by someone other than the person who built it.

**Fail if:** localhost only with no offline fallback; the happy path is a screenshot; or the demo cannot be shown if the venue Wi-Fi dies.

## 2. Weighted criteria — 0 / 1 / 3 / 5 anchors

Score only 0, 1, 3 or 5. "Pretty good" is a 3.

### Actionability — 30%

*Official test: "Are the improvement proposals concrete enough for a health centre or wellbeing services county to implement?" The brief also says this is "the most important part."*

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| **0** | No recommendations, or only problem diagnosis / "more research needed". |
| **1** | Generic advice ("improve referrals", "detect earlier", "start treatment faster") with no owner, place, date, cost or KPI. Or a national wish-list a county cannot start alone. |
| **3** | At least one action with who / what / where / when / KPI, and a plausible link to the roadmap, but cost/effort is vague, the action is still too big for a Monday start, or it is not tied to the demonstrated gap. |
| **5** | A named county or health-centre can start next week. Owner, setting, 30- or 90-day first step, effort or cost band, and a measurable target on a Goal 3 indicator. The action is mapped to a numbered roadmap toimenpide (e.g. 3.1.2 area-level care pathways, 2.5.2 standardised referral criteria — the latter is costed inside 3.1.1, roadmap p. 106). "Beyond Junction" is a concrete follow-up ask to one county, not a slogan. |

### Insight — 25%

*Official test: "Does the solution reveal a real and meaningful gap, and show where it emerges in the care pathway?"*

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| **0** | No gap, or a gap invented without data. National rhetoric only ("cancer is increasing"). |
| **1** | National averages, or a difference that is not located on the pathway (detection / referral from primary to specialist care / diagnosis / treatment initiation). Or the "gap" is a well-known fact with no magnitude and no regional contrast. |
| **3** | A real, sourced regional or socioeconomic difference, but the pathway step is inferred rather than shown, the magnitude is modest or poorly compared, or several bottlenecks are listed so the story has no centre. |
| **5** | One specific gap, with magnitude, for named counties or groups, located at **one** pathway step, with a clear "this is where it opens" argument. Alternative explanations are considered (age structure, case mix, data missingness). The insight changes what a director would do on Monday. |

### Data quality & transparency — 20%

*Official test: "Are the sources clear, limitations acknowledged, and findings free from unsupported assumptions?" The brief itself says cancer-care data in Finland is limited, and that addressing this data gap is part of the challenge.*

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| **0** | Unsourced or fabricated numbers; synthetic data shown as real; sources not recoverable. |
| **1** | Some sources, but mixed with unsourced claims; methods opaque; limitations missing; ecological or small-number problems ignored; AI text treated as a citation. |
| **3** | Sources for the main claims, a limitations note, no fake statistics — but citations are hard to see in the demo, uncertainty is not shown (no ranges, no "n too small"), or a causal leap is made from aggregate data. |
| **5** | Every on-screen number is sourced. Methods (indicator definition, years, regions, exclusions) are one click away. Limitations and data gaps are spoken out loud. Placeholders are labelled. Mentors vs public data are distinguished. The team does not claim more than the data can bear — and uses that honesty as part of the product (e.g. "here is what a county cannot yet see; here is the official 3.2 quality-register gap"). |

### Execution — 15%

*Official test: "Does the solution work in a live demonstration in front of the judges?"*

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| **0** | Nothing runs. Deck only. Or a crash with no backup. |
| **1** | Something runs on one laptop, fragile, demo script only, no offline plan; or a video is the real demo. |
| **3** | Live demo works on the happy path in under two minutes, but it is brittle, looks unfinished, requires a hidden Wi-Fi trick, or does not show the recommendation and the source in the same flow. |
| **5** | Live, offline-safe, rehearsed under 90 seconds, no crash, backup recording ready, a second person can drive it. The demo *is* the story: gap → pathway step → Monday action → Goal 3 KPI. Public URL as a bonus, not a dependency. |

### Clarity — 10%

*Official test: "Can a decision-maker understand the key message and proposed action within two minutes?" The brief: "A strong solution should make a complex situation clear and actionable for a non-technical decision-maker in less than two minutes."*

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| **0** | Incomprehensible, or a model-architecture tour. |
| **1** | A dashboard walkthrough; the action is at the end if at all; jargon (ICD, Cox, "our LLM agentic pipeline") in the first minute; no single sentence a director could repeat. |
| **3** | Message is understandable, but it takes more than two minutes, or the ask is fuzzy, or too many charts compete with the action. |
| **5** | In two minutes a non-technical director can repeat: the gap, where it sits, what to do Monday, and how they will know it worked. One name, one indicator, one ask. Closing line links to Beyond Junction (a follow-up with a Finnish wellbeing services county). |

## 3. Review checklists by team output

Use these when scoring work as it lands in `data/`, `recs/` and `app/`. Tick every box or write the fix. A missing box is not a "nice to have".

### 3.1 `data/` — gap, pathway location, sources

- [ ] A single primary gap is stated in one sentence, with magnitude and comparator (county vs county, or county vs national / Nordic), not a list of interesting charts.
- [ ] The gap is located at exactly one pathway step: detection, referral (primary → specialist), diagnosis, or treatment start. If the evidence is ambiguous, the write-up says so and still picks a centre for the pitch.
- [ ] The chosen Goal 3 indicator is named, quoted, and used as the north-star KPI. If the sharpest descriptive gap is a Goal 2 measure (e.g. stage I–II), a Goal 3 indicator is still computed and shown.
- [ ] `SOURCES.md` (or equivalent) lists every dataset: publisher, year, geography, indicator id, URL, extraction date, licence.
- [ ] No invented numbers. Every placeholder is labelled `EXAMPLE` / `PLACEHOLDER`.
- [ ] Limitations are explicit: lag, coverage, missing well-being services counties, small counts, age standardisation yes/no, what the data cannot say about individual patients (brief forbids patient-level data).
- [ ] Alternative explanations are tested or flagged (age structure, incidence mix, coding, travel to another county for treatment).
- [ ] A director can see "their" county without being shamed with unsourced league-table theatrics.

**Harsh fail triggers:** national averages only; pathway step not shown; Goal 2 passed off as Goal 3; unpublished numbers in a pitch chart.

### 3.2 `recs/` — actions a county can take

- [ ] Each headline action has who, what, where, when, expected effect, KPI.
- [ ] Each action maps to a numbered roadmap toimenpide, with page (Goal 3 = roadmap pp. 54–76; 3.1 pathways pp. 54–58; 3.2 quality register pp. 59–; 3.4 functional-capacity / elderly pp. 68–; referral criteria 2.5.2 is on pp. 51–53 and costed under 3.1.1, annex p. 106).
- [ ] Cost or effort band is given, preferably using the official annex figures where they exist (do not invent a ROI). Note: the roadmap (p. 102) says Goal 3 *savings* are only partly estimated (19 M€, mainly palliative care) because the knowledge base is still weak — do not fabricate Goal 3 savings.
- [ ] Actions are sized for a health centre or a wellbeing services county, not only for STM or a future national register that does not yet exist. National actions can be supporting, not the Monday step.
- [ ] Evidence for "this could work" is cited (roadmap, Nordic pathway models named in the roadmap — Sweden's SVF, Denmark's Kræftpakkeforløb, Norway's Pakkeforløp, roadmap p. 56 — or mentor insight). No unsourced "AI will cut waiting times by 30%".
- [ ] Goal 3 mapping is a table, not a paragraph of adjectives.
- [ ] One primary action is marked as the pitch action; the rest are a backup longlist.

**Harsh fail triggers:** analysis dressed up as recs; no owner; no KPI; cost pulled from thin air; ten equal actions and no priority.

### 3.3 `app/` — live demo the judges will actually watch

- [ ] Public URL *and* an offline-safe local path are documented. Venue Wi-Fi is assumed to fail.
- [ ] A 90-second demo script exists: hook → gap (one number) → pathway step → Monday card → source + limitation → Beyond Junction ask.
- [ ] The Goal 3 indicator quote and page number are visible in the UI, not only in a docs file.
- [ ] Every number in the UI has a source control (tooltip, footer, or "i" panel). `EXAMPLE` labels are impossible to miss.
- [ ] The recommendation is a first-class object (who / what / where / when / KPI / cost band), not a chatbot afterthought.
- [ ] A second team member has driven the demo once. Backup recording (silent, same script) is in the repo or on the laptop.
- [ ] Feature freeze discipline: no live model calls required for the judged path, unless they degrade gracefully.
- [ ] The first screen is for a county director, not for a data scientist.

**Harsh fail triggers:** demo is a dashboard tour; sources hidden; crash on first click; slides substituted for the product.

### Usual point-losers (re-check before 14:00)

- Synthetic or "illustrative" numbers not labelled as such.
- AI that only restates charts and does not propose an evidence-tied action.
- No answer to "how would a county start next month, and what would it cost?"
- No link to AstraZeneca's "Beyond Junction" follow-up with a wellbeing services county.
- Detection-only story that forgets Goal 3.
- More than two minutes before the action is said out loud.

### 4.1 What Goal 3 *is*

The strategy has four päätavoitteet. Goal 3 is stated on the summary page and again as a chapter title:

> "3. Varmistetaan yhdenvertainen ja vaikuttava syövänhoito"  
> — strategy p. 3 (Yhteenveto) and p. 32 (chapter opening); same wording in the seurantamittarit figure, p. 5, and osatavoitteet figure, p. 13.

Chapter framing, p. 32 (compressed, not a substitute for the PDF): Finland's survival figures are internationally comparable, but there are still significant development needs in how care is organised; regional differences in resources, practices and support services weaken equity; national guidelines and well-coordinated care pathways, with timely access to care, are what guarantee effective, equal and timely treatment.

### 4.2 Official Goal 3 *seurantamittarit* (the ones that satisfy G2 most cleanly)

From **Kuva 1**, strategy **p. 5**, under the heading "Varmistetaan yhdenvertainen ja vaikuttava syövänhoito":

1. **"Huonoennusteisten syöpien 5 vuoden elossaololuvut saavuttavat pohjoismaisen tason"**  
   Footnote `**` on the same page: "Viiden vuoden suhteellinen elossaolo kasvaa Nordcan-tilastossa esitetystä vuoden 2023 tasosta yli 14 prosenttiyksikköä keuhkosyövässä, 6 prosenttiyksikköä haimasyövässä ja 14 prosenttiyksikköä maksasyövässä. Pohjoismaisella tasolla viitataan Ruotsin ja Norjan keskiarvoon."

2. **"Syöpäkuolleisuus pienenee 10 % (nykyisin 13 300 henkilöä vuodessa)"**

3. **"Potilaat pääsevät hoitoon tavoiteaikojen puitteissa"**  
   Footnote `***` on the same page: "THL (Ohjaus 21/2016). Hoitoonpääsyn seuranta syövän hoidossa."

These three are the headline Goal 3 indicators. For a two-minute county-director story, **indicator 3 (treatment start / access within target times)** is the most actionable. Indicators 1–2 are outcome measures that move slowly and need careful case-mix language.

### 4.3 Cross-cutting yhdenvertaisuus indicator (p. 5) — useful, not a Goal 3 substitute

> "Alueelliset ja sosioekonomiset erot syövän ilmaantuvuudessa, hoidossa ja syöpäkuolleisuudessa ovat käytettävissä palvelutoiminnassa ja niillä ohjataan toimintaa"

This sits in Kuva 1 under "Yhdenvertaisuus", described in the p. 5 intro as a theme that cuts across all goals. It is highly relevant to an equity pitch, but **do not use it alone to pass G2**. Pair it with one of the three Goal 3 bullets above.

### 4.4 Do not confuse with Goal 2 (common trap)

Also on p. 5, under "Vähennetään syöpätaakkaa ehkäisyn ja varhaisen toteamisen avulla":

- "Ehkäistävien syöpien määrä laskee 1000 tapauksella vuodessa"
- "Kaikissa syöpäseulontaohjelmissa saavutetaan 80 %:n osallistumisaste jokaisella hyvinvointialueella"
- "Vähintään 70 % syövistä diagnosoidaan levinneisyysluokissa I-II"

These support a *detection* story, but they are **Goal 2**. If the data team's sharpest finding is late stage at diagnosis, still put a Goal 3 mittari (usually hoitoonpääsy) on the same screen.

### 4.5 Sub-goal measurement bullets (additional Goal 3 mittarit)

The challenge says "at least one indicator connected to Goal 3". The sub-goal measurement lists below are also Goal 3-connected and may be used, with page citations. Prefer a Kuva 1 headline indicator plus, if needed, one sub-goal mittari.

**3.1 Kansalliset hoitosuositukset ja hoitoketjut…** (strategy p. 33) — "Tavoitteen toteutumista mitataan seuraavasti:"

- "Ajantasaisten kansallisten hoitosuositusten ja hoitoketjujen saatavuudessa ollaan pohjoismaisella tasolla (hoitosuositusten osuus suhteessa pohjoismaisiin verrokkeihin, %)."
- "Potilaat pääsevät hoitoon tavoiteaikojen puitteissa."
- "Kansalliset ja yhtenäiset tavoitteet ja keskeisimmät vaikuttavuuden seurantamittarit määritetty sovittuihin syöpiin."

**3.2 Kansallinen syövänhoidon laaturekisteri…** (strategy p. 36)

- "Syöpäkohtaisten tietojen kirjaaminen laaturekisteriin, vertaiskehittämistoiminta käytössä (syöpätyyppien lukumäärä, joista tieto käytettävissä laaturekistereissä)."
- "Laaturekisterin tietopohja (ml. levinneisyystieto) mahdollistaa laadun ja vaikuttavuuden luotettavan arvioinnin ja vertailun (tietopohjan kattavuus 90 %)."
- "Saatavilla on luotettavaa tietoa syövän diagnostiikka- ja hoitokäytäntöjen alueellisista eroista, minkä avulla voidaan kaventaa eroja hoitotuloksissa."
- "Kliinisten syöpäilmoitusten määrä (tavoitteena 90 % tapauksista automatisoidusti vuoteen 2030 mennessä)."

The 3.2 list is the honest frame for our data-gap: the strategy itself says a national cancer-care quality register does not yet exist (p. 35).

**3.3 Kuntoutus, psykososiaalinen tuki…** (strategy p. 38) — share of patients with symptom/QoL/function data; share of units with psychosocial and rehab services; share with an individual rehab plan; palliative-planning and last-month-of-life treatment indicators (full bullets on p. 38).

**3.4 Toimintakykytieto ohjaa ikääntyneiden…** (strategy p. 39)

- Share of wellbeing services counties where all eligible older cancer patients receive a geriatric-oncology assessment.
- Share of counties where primary care can consult a geriatric oncology team already at the diagnostic stage.
- Share of cancer-care units that carry out geriatric screening and assessment of older patients.
- Use of the G8 screen and/or a geriatrician's presence in MDT, with monitoring of G8 use.

**3.5 Riittävä ja osaava henkilöstö…** (strategy p. 42)

- Regional distribution of professionals, sick leave and job-satisfaction surveys.
- Annual numbers completing qualification / specialist training.
- Geriatric oncology included in geriatric specialist training.
- A specialist training programme in palliative medicine established.
- Uptake of digital care pathways in cancer-care units.

### 4.6 Goal 3 osatavoitteet (for mapping actions, p. 13 and p. 32)

- 3.1 National treatment guidelines and care pathways steer equal, timely and effective care (pp. 33–34).
- 3.2 A national quality register, systematic review of results and peer review support improvement (pp. 35–36).
- 3.3 Rehab, psychosocial support, health social work and palliative care are an established, equally available part of the pathway (pp. 37–38).
- 3.4 Functional-capacity information steers diagnostics and treatment of older cancer patients (pp. 39–40).
- 3.5 Sufficient and skilled staff secure effective, people-centred cancer care (pp. 41–42).
